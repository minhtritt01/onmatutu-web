Five in the afternoon, and the phone buzzes again. He doesn't need to open it to know what
it says: another favour. In that office he's the easy one to ask — he says yes to everything,
and only afterwards remembers he still has three unfinished files of his own.

![Moment 1](/characters/03-09-2026-van-phong-1.png)

His fingers type "sure, let me…" before his head catches up. Then he stops. He looks at the
two stacks on the desk: other people's work piled high, his own thin and pushed to the very
edge. Nobody is forcing him — nobody ever was. That yes is just a habit he's kept so long he
forgot it could be put down. He deletes the line and writes a different one.

![Moment 2](/characters/03-09-2026-van-phong-2.png)

After he hits send, his heart pounds for a few minutes. He sits still, afraid of being
thought selfish, afraid someone will be angry. Then the reply comes: two words, they'll ask
someone else, that's it. No anger, nothing. The anger he sat there waiting for was never on
the other end — it was built right where he was sitting.

![Moment 3](/characters/03-09-2026-van-phong-3.png)

At six he walks out of the building. He turns around and the whole floor has been dark for
hours — so no one has been keeping him there these past months. He was keeping himself. The
road home still has light on it, the kind of light he hasn't seen in a long time.

> How long since I saw the sky still light.
> Tonight I got home in time to look.

Back in his room, the laptop bag leans against the wall and stays there. No announcement. When
was the last time you said no?
