Ninety days, and he hadn't done a single thing wrong. She knew the number exactly, because she
had been counting. Two years since the divorce, three months of someone being patient with her,
and what she did every night wasn't asking herself whether she cared about him.

![Moment 1](/characters/15-08-2026-tinh-cam-1.png)

There's a note in her phone. Every time he was kind, she wrote a line down — not to remember it,
but to save it. So that later, when it all fell apart, she'd have proof she had seen everything
coming. It sounds like carefulness. But being careful and having already passed sentence are two
different things. The trouble is her ex-husband was good too, and stayed good for six years. So
no number was ever going to save her. Ninety days, and then what. Six years, and then what.

![Moment 2](/characters/15-08-2026-tinh-cam-2.png)

One evening he showed up ten minutes late. The first thing out of her mouth was an apology. She
heard herself say it and couldn't catch it in time — she'd rehearsed that sentence for six years,
in a different kitchen, for a different man. The person across the table just looked confused and
said: you didn't do anything. It turned out nobody at this table had asked her for an apology at
all. The person she was afraid of running into was never her ex-husband. It was herself, the
version that had learned to bow first.

![Moment 3](/characters/15-08-2026-tinh-cam-3.png)

Before that she had cancelled on him three times. The dress had been hanging in her closet since
the first cancellation, still in its garment bag. She kept thinking cancelling was her own
business, that nobody lost anything. Until she found out he had booked a table every single time.
Three evenings that table was held, lit, and empty. Keeping your distance protects no one — it
just splits the loss in two.

> That chair isn't your ex.
> You decide who gets to sit.

She didn't rush into anything. She just took the dress down and sent one short message: seven
o'clock. Some things really don't heal in a night — but are you making someone pay for what
somebody else did?
