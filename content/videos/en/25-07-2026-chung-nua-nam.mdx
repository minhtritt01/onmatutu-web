There's a July calendar on the wall, more than half its squares already crossed out. He looks at it for a while, then asks himself the question nobody else is going to ask: half the year is gone — what do I have to show for it?

![Moment 1](/characters/25-07-2026-chung-4.png)

He opens his phone. His friends' stories scroll past: someone got a raise, someone just came back from a trip. His thumb stops on his own camera roll — a photo from March, the one he likes best, and the one he never posted. He closes the app and sits still for a minute. Not sad exactly. Just a beat behind everyone else.

![Moment 2](/characters/25-07-2026-chung-5.png)

Some people lost more than that. In January he had a job; now he doesn't. Looking back at these six months, there's nothing he feels able to say out loud — and when his mother calls to ask how work is going, his throat tightens and he says "I'm fine, mom" anyway. After hanging up he opens his laptop and starts fixing a CV he hasn't touched in months. Nobody knows what time of night he does this.

![Moment 3](/characters/25-07-2026-chung-6.png)

Then a friend texts: "so what's the plan now?" The rest of the year is out there waiting, and he has no plan at all. He sits with it until the tea goes cold and he still hasn't replied — because what's actually holding him up isn't not knowing. It's not having an answer that sounds impressive enough.

> I'm afraid my answer won't be good enough.
> Not afraid of not knowing yet.

In the end he replies with exactly what's true, then goes and pours fresh tea. No comeback story, no miracle to report. The next six months are coming either way, at their own steady pace, whether or not you have an answer ready — so what are you still holding back from posting?
