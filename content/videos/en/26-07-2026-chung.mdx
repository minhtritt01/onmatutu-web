His brother left school at 18. He got his degree at 22. Between those two dates sit ten years of his brother working without complaining once, without asking for anything of his own. The whole family came to the graduation and took a hundred photos. That night, scrolling back through them, he noticed something odd.

![Moment 1](/characters/26-07-2026-chung-1.png)

His brother isn't in any of them. Not just today — not in ten years of them. He was always the one holding the camera. Birthdays, Tết, the day the acceptance letter came, the day the degree did. Everyone smiled into the lens, and nobody ever thought to trade places with the man standing behind it. So he took the camera and told his brother to step in. His brother scratched his head, laughed, said don't bother.

![Moment 2](/characters/26-07-2026-chung-2.png)

Later that night he saw his brother's wallet. The same leather one from back when he was in high school — edges gone soft, stitching split at one corner. Meanwhile the laptop his brother had just bought him for his new job still smelled like the box. It isn't that his brother can't afford a new wallet. He stopped being poor years ago. It's that ten years of counting every đồng for two younger siblings became a habit he can't put down.

![Moment 3](/characters/26-07-2026-chung-3.png)

At 11pm he sent a proper thank-you — the first one in ten years. The reply came back short: "Just go get a job. No need to thank me." He sat looking at the screen for a long while before he understood why it was brushed off so fast. Accepting thanks would quietly turn his brother into a creditor and him into a debtor, and his brother didn't want him carrying that.

> Ten years of family photos, one person missing.
> The one who paid for every single frame.

Some people love you in a way that makes sure you never notice it happening. In your family, who is that?
