Twenty-four, fourth year away from home. Four people in that rented room, none of them related — just
four names on one lease. There are always four pairs of sandals by the door, never lined up straight.
Inside, four thin mats laid close enough that when one person turns over at night, the other three
know.

![Moment 1](/characters/08-08-2026-chung-1.png)

Stay away from home long enough and something happens that nobody warns you about: you stop keeping
your own birthday. Not out of sadness — it just stops being for anything. That night the light went
out. A small cake came out, the cheapest kind from the shop at the mouth of the alley — and the stack
of empty paper coffee cups that had been sitting on the table all week suddenly meant something. He
had forgotten himself. The other three hadn't.

![Moment 2](/characters/08-08-2026-chung-2.png)

There's a cost to being far from home that people rarely name: you can be sick for three days and
nobody back home knows. It isn't hiding, exactly. What would calling accomplish — his mother can't
fly down. At three in the morning there was a knock and a bowl of rice porridge. The one who brought
it still had to be up at five for his shift. He knew what to do because he had never called his own
family either.

![Moment 3](/characters/08-08-2026-chung-3.png)

By the end of the month everyone's wallet is empty, and nobody says so out loud. Yet the rice jar in
the kitchen is never empty, and beside it sit four condensed-milk cans used for measuring. Nobody
proposed it, nobody takes credit. Every 28th, each of them quietly tips his share in, and by evening
the pot is as full as any other night.

> Nobody here shares my last name.
> Someone still remembered me.

You don't get to choose who you're related to. But that room, those four pairs of sandals, those four
bowls on the tray — every one of them is somebody who chose to stay. And the family you picked for
yourself: who's in it right now?
