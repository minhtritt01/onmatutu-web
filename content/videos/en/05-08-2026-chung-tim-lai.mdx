The box had sat in the corner of the wardrobe for two years. The tape had yellowed, and the dust on
the lid was thick enough that wiping it left a handprint. She knew what was inside — the thing she
used to pull out every night back then, working at it until late, doing it because she wanted to
and for no one else. She just hadn't opened it once in two years.

![Moment 1](/characters/05-08-2026-chung-4.png)

Even after cleaning it off, her hands couldn't remember where to start. She'd been telling everyone
she was busy lately. But standing there, holding it, the truth was plain: she hadn't run out of
time. She was the one who put it away, wrapped it, taped it shut, pushed it into the corner. And
put away, along with it, the part of her that could be glad about small things.

![Moment 2](/characters/05-08-2026-chung-5.png)

Every evening of those two years looked the same: get home, lie down, scroll until her eyes gave
out. It wasn't that the joy had gone. It was that no room had been left for the thing she liked —
and that room doesn't appear on its own; you have to leave it open. Tonight she put the phone down
half an hour early. Just half an hour.

![Moment 3](/characters/05-08-2026-chung-6.png)

Picking it up again, her hands were stiff, and she got it wrong at exactly the part she used to be
best at. Halfway through, her eyes stung — not from sadness, but because it turned out she had
missed it more than she thought. She stayed another half hour, kept getting it wrong, and didn't
get up.

> It isn't angry that you left.
> It's just been there, waiting.

The thing you loved back then — where is it sitting in your home right now?
