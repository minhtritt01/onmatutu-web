The 5th of the month. Early morning in a rented room, a green tick on the phone screen:
rent transferred. The light through the window is still pale, the work shirt hangs on the
door hook, the alarm went off at five-thirty. The holiday is over. Today it's traffic
again, meetings again, coming home tired again — exactly like every day before the break.

![Moment 1](/characters/14-09-2026-chung-1.png)

He opens his feed: beaches, cafés, other people's trips. His own day has nothing worth
posting: wake up early, sit in traffic, sit in meetings, eat, sleep. Boring enough that he
wouldn't bother telling anyone. Then he looks back at the green tick. This month's rent —
that boring rhythm paid it. Twenty-two working days with nothing to show off, and every
month it's enough. The routine he's bored of is the one feeding him. He closes the app
and takes the bike to work.

![Moment 2](/characters/14-09-2026-chung-2.png)

That night his mother texts: got it, son. Two million đồng, sent every month, never
mentioned to anyone. He scrolls through his transfer history — every row looks like every
other row, none of them stands out. And suddenly he sees that the two million isn't money.
It's twenty-two days of early alarms, traffic and meetings that he still calls boring.
This boring rhythm is feeding his mother too. He replies with a single "yes, Mom", puts
the phone face-down, sets the alarm for six.

![Moment 3](/characters/14-09-2026-chung-3.png)

Some nights he sits on the sofa, phone dark on the armrest, and wonders: is this life too
plain? A friend texts "how've you been" and he has nothing to answer. No good news, no bad
news. Then he looks at the empty notification tray, the stack of paid bills pinned to the
wall. Having nothing to tell also means owing no one, nothing broken. Steady isn't plain.
It's peace. He sets the alarm, same as every day.

> Not worth posting online.
> Worth living, every single day.

Not everyone needs a brilliant life. A lot of people just need one that runs steady —
work to go to, sleep that comes, enough money at month's end, a small good thing now and
then. Is that rhythm of yours still running this week?
