The door had been shut since that afternoon. Behind it, a seventeen-year-old who'd skipped school a couple of times that week, talked back to his parents, and tonight wouldn't come out for dinner. Outside it, a round of shouting that had just ended — after it the whole house went quiet, and the door sat tighter shut than before.

His older brother, twenty-six, didn't add to it. He just stood there, knocked a few times, and set a plate of food down on the floor.

![Moment 1](/characters/05-08-2026-chung-1.png)

A few knocks, and not one of them got an answer. He wasn't angry, and he never explained to anyone why he was standing at a door nobody was going to open. He just remembered being seventeen himself, locking that same kind of door — and knowing that what sat behind it wasn't defiance. Shouting has never opened a door. So instead of knocking again, he left the food and sat down right there in the hallway.

![Moment 2](/characters/05-08-2026-chung-2.png)

Half an hour in, the door was still closed and he was still sitting outside it. Then his brother started talking — a few sentences, broken up: the schoolwork was too much, and nobody at school would sit with him anymore. When he finished, he asked something the older brother hadn't expected: "Are you mad at me?" So the silence all evening had never been stubbornness. He'd stayed quiet because speaking up meant getting shouted at again. His brother shook his head, and stayed a while longer.

![Moment 3](/characters/05-08-2026-chung-3.png)

By late that night he still hadn't turned back into the son their parents are waiting for — one evening doesn't do that. Earlier the knock had gone unanswered, and his brother had assumed he was being tuned out. Then the knock came the other way — his brother at his door, asking if he wanted to watch something. Partway through the movie he mentioned he'd heard every single knock. The silence that had felt like being ignored was someone sitting on the other side of the door, listening to all of it.

> I couldn't fix him.
> I just didn't leave him alone in there.

One evening doesn't fix anyone, and maybe he was never the one meant to. He only showed up, long enough and quietly enough that when his brother was finally ready, somebody was already sitting there.

Is there a door in your house that's been closed a while, that nobody knocks on anymore?
