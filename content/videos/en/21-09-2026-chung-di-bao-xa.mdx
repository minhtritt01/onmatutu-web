The bank notification says the salary is in. The first thing he does, like every month, is open
the app and send his mom two million. Then he sits looking at what's left, and without meaning
to, he starts counting: three years of work, and not a single đồng saved.

![Moment 1](/characters/21-09-2026-chung-4.png)

Counted that way, it's disheartening. After mom's share there's just enough to eat, every month
the same, nothing to show anyone. But that night he scrolls back through old messages and finds
a chat from three years ago: "Mom, can you send me some money?" Back then he was the one asking.
The balance now is about the same as the balance then — only the direction of the money has
flipped. From the kid being fed, he's become the one feeding his mother back. He looks at the
number again, and it doesn't hurt anymore.

![Moment 2](/characters/21-09-2026-chung-5.png)

Near midnight, his youngest brother calls. The kid moved to the city a few weeks ago; he's
crying, saying he wants to go home. He holds the phone and almost says the honest thing: I've
been here three years and I've got nothing either, what am I supposed to tell you. Then he hears
his brother cry and realizes: that's exactly how he cried three years ago, same voice, same
"I want to go home." Back then he was the one calling. Now he's the one listening. He tells his
brother: I've been there — and I'm still here.

![Moment 3](/characters/21-09-2026-chung-6.png)

Another night, he runs a fever. Puts on a jacket, walks to the pharmacy alone, comes back and
lies down alone, feeling low because this year nothing was saved, nothing was gained, and even
being sick is something he does by himself. Then he remembers: three years ago a fever meant
calling mom in tears, and mom losing a whole night's sleep back home. Tonight he takes the
medicine and texts her two words: "I'm fine." Mom sleeps through. Nothing to count, but something
has quietly changed.

> Back then a fever kept mom awake.
> Now she sleeps. That's how I know I've grown.

Count what you don't have yet and every year comes up short. But count from where you started —
what you were afraid of three years ago, who you asked, who you called late at night — and how
far have you actually come?
