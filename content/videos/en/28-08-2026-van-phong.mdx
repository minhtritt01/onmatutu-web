Five years, and she has never liked this job. Not hated it — hating something would at least be easy to name. It's just that every morning she opens the file and feels nothing at all.

![Moment 1](/characters/28-08-2026-van-phong-1.png)

She works fast, so the hard tasks all find their way to her desk. Then one day her boss said it: you were born to do this. It sounded like praise, but something got tighter after that — once you're the person born for the job, there's no room left for being tired of it. Everyone tells her she's good. Nobody has asked if she likes it.

![Moment 2](/characters/28-08-2026-van-phong-2.png)

Some mornings she sits in front of the screen with a coffee she poured early and never touched. No excitement, no dread. She used to think that was lucky — at least she isn't miserable. But if you hate something, you at least know what you want instead. Not hating it is the harder part, because there's no trail to follow.

![Moment 3](/characters/28-08-2026-van-phong-3.png)

That night she opened a job board, left it there, and closed it. This salary is still paying for her younger sibling's school, she likes to say. But if she sits with it a little longer, she knows the truth: ask her what she'd switch to, and she has no answer. In the end she didn't quit. She just circled two empty squares on the week's calendar — two evenings that belong to no one else.

> Not being able to change careers yet is fine.
> Every week still has a part that's mine.

Some seasons, nothing changes at all. But those two empty squares — those she gets to keep. Which square did you leave open for yourself this week?
