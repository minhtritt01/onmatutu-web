Eleven at night. Her light was off, but a thin strip of yellow still showed under the door. On the other side of the wall, her parents were working out this semester's tuition. She lay still, pulled the blanket up, and kept her breathing even, like someone asleep.

![Moment 1](/characters/06-08-2026-chung-4.png)

Her mother said if it's short, I'll find a way. Her father went quiet for a while, then said let me work it out, we may have to borrow. She heard every word — and the strange part wasn't that their voices rose. It was that both of them were competing to take the harder half. Her mother claimed the scrambling, her father claimed the debt. An argument with both people on the same side. In the morning she asked nothing.

![Moment 2](/characters/06-08-2026-chung-5.png)

At breakfast she tried to ask for one semester off, to work and save and come back after. She hadn't finished the sentence before her mother shook her head. Fast enough that she understood: that option had already been weighed and thrown out, right there in last night's argument. The quick refusal wasn't her mother not listening — it was the tail end of something already settled, so her daughter wouldn't have to weigh it again.

![Moment 3](/characters/06-08-2026-chung-6.png)

A few days later she took an evening shift and told no one at home, because saying it out loud would mean her mother stopping her. They argued at night and smiled over breakfast like nothing happened — she didn't invent that trick of hiding. She learned it from them.

> Nobody in this house says it out loud.
> That's how all of us keep going.

Some families love each other by making sure the other person never has to see them straining. Is there something you're keeping from your parents right now?
