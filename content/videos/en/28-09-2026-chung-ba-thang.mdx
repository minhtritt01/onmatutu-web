The calendar on the wall has flipped to October. He stands looking at it for a while, then at the
things still unfinished: each one started, each one only halfway there. Three months left in the year.

![Moment 1](/characters/28-09-2026-chung-4.png)

His first instinct is to catch up on all of it. He fills every slot of the week. Then he remembers
doing exactly this at the start of the year — taking on everything at once, keen for a few weeks,
and ending up with nothing finished. Doing too much means finishing nothing. So this time he crosses
things off and keeps just one.

![Moment 2](/characters/28-09-2026-chung-5.png)

The one he keeps is a course he paid for long ago and barely started. Every time he opened it, he
told himself he was too busy — next month. But when he actually does the maths, a few evenings a
week for three months is enough to finish. The time was always there. He just hadn't opened the
lesson.

![Moment 3](/characters/28-09-2026-chung-6.png)

Some evenings he comes home from work worn out and thinks about skipping a session. Just one. But
he knows the last course he dropped started with exactly one skipped session like this. So he sits
down, opens the book, and finishes the session — not because he feels like it, but because the
feeling only comes once he's at the desk.

> Choosing one thing isn't giving up.
> It's letting one thing get finished.

There are three months left this year. If you could finish only one thing, which would it be?
