That morning she opened her laptop with her head still somewhere else. Someone at home had been
in the hospital all week, so she had been going back and forth, and the work had stacked up into
something she hadn't touched. And yet the screen came up and it was already finished.

![Moment 1](/characters/04-09-2026-van-phong-1.png)

She had asked around before that. This person, then that person — everyone genuinely was busy.
Nobody had lied to her. So when she saw the work done, her first instinct was to find out who.
She asked the whole room; nobody claimed it. Only later did she learn it was the one sitting
farthest away, the quietest one, the only person she had never thought to ask. Her round of
asking hadn't just failed — it had picked the wrong people from the very first step.

![Moment 2](/characters/04-09-2026-van-phong-2.png)

She went over to say thank you. The answer was one short sentence: last year her own mother was
sick, she had been in exactly this position, and someone had helped her then. Then she turned
back to her screen and said nothing more. So it wasn't a gift at all. It was a repayment — of
something received a year ago, paid forward rather than back.

![Moment 3](/characters/04-09-2026-van-phong-3.png)

Sitting down again at her own desk, she looked around the room she had always thought of as a
place where everyone was busy and everyone was competing. The kind people had been there the
whole time. She had simply been ranking her colleagues by how close she felt to them, and that
ranking had been wrong from the start.

> She must have stood where I'm standing.
> Now it's my turn.

The next day she saw an empty desk beside her with work still sitting on it. She did it, and told
no one. If you had to name the kindest person in your own office right now — would you name the
right one?
