Tonight there is nothing to wait for. He notices it when he opens the wall calendar out of
habit — the habit of all of August, crossing off one square every evening, counting how many
days until the holiday. Now the September page is blank. Nothing circled. Tết is five months
away.

![Moment 1](/characters/11-09-2026-chung-1.png)

He lies there, bored, with no idea what to do. He flips the calendar forward to January and
circles Tết, just to have a mark to look at — and immediately sees what he's doing: counting
again. After Tết it will feel exactly like tonight, because this emptiness was never about the
holiday ending. It's about only ever living at the milestones and throwing away the few hundred
days in between. He puts on a jacket and walks around the block.

![Moment 2](/characters/11-09-2026-chung-2.png)

The first night after the holiday, he scrolls, turns the screen off, scrolls again. Not sad.
Not happy. Just empty — the emptiness of someone who has crossed the finish line and hasn't
drawn a new one. On the nightstand is the bus ticket home, corner torn. A whole month with a
single goal, and that goal is now a used slip of paper. He turns the phone off and reaches for
the book on the shelf.

![Moment 3](/characters/11-09-2026-chung-3.png)

At his usual café, it hits him: he remembers every hour of the four-day holiday and not a
single day of the thirty-one in August. Those thirty-one days were a waiting room. Tonight he
isn't counting anything, just reading a half-finished book under a warm bulb — and it feels
fuller than the entire month before it. He opens a notebook and writes one line for Monday:
come back here.

> Every holiday ends eventually.
> The ordinary days are the ones you actually have.

The next holiday is exactly as far away as it was. Only tonight is different. What are you
counting down to — and where are you keeping the days in between?
