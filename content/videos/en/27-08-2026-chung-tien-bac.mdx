"Money can't buy happiness" is usually said by someone who isn't worrying about money. He's counting down to payday, and every time his phone buzzes with an invitation to eat out, he has to invent a reason that sounds natural.

![Moment 1](/characters/27-08-2026-chung-1.png)

This time the reason is "busy". He sends it, puts the phone face down, and sits still for a while in a room whose only company is the calendar on the wall. The expensive part isn't the meal he skipped — it's the lesson he just taught himself: being short on money is something you hide. Fifteen minutes later his friend texts back. He'd just cancelled too. Also broke. The two of them had been hiding the same thing, one room each.

![Moment 2](/characters/27-08-2026-chung-2.png)

Another day, he stands at the pharmacy counter for twenty minutes over a cough that's lasted three weeks. Picks up the box, looks at the price, puts it back down. In that moment he feels small — not because anyone said anything, but because he passed sentence on himself before anyone had a chance to look. Then the pharmacist, without asking a single question, slides over a cheaper paper packet. From start to finish only one person in that shop was judging him, and it wasn't her.

![Moment 3](/characters/27-08-2026-chung-3.png)

And there are nights he scrolls his phone and sees someone his own age showing off a new car. Not envy — just the sense of being slow, as if life came with a schedule he's fallen behind on. Until the other person adds one more line: three years without taking a day off. He isn't ahead. He's just paying in a different currency.

> Your balance isn't your worth.
> It's only this month's number.

Nobody has both time and money at once, and if you're counting every coin this week, plenty of people are doing the same — they just don't post about it. If someone asked you right now what you're paying for this stage of your life with, what would you say?
