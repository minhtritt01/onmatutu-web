Thirty. Not the best in the room — he knows that without anyone pointing it out. The new hire sits one desk over, his screen full of lines while his own is half done. Home at night, desk lamp on, book open, studying until late. The wall clock says almost midnight. Tired, still sitting there.

![Moment 1](/characters/16-09-2026-van-phong-1.png)

Then one day a hard job comes in. It isn't the fastest one who gets the call. The phone lights up on his desk. The client calls him — because he works carefully, rarely slips, and when it's done, it's done. The slowness he kept punishing himself for turns out to be the thing people trust. That night he closes the book earlier than usual. Not giving up on studying. Just nothing left to prove tonight.

![Moment 2](/characters/16-09-2026-van-phong-2.png)

There were two years of chasing the younger ones. Three empty coffee cups on the desk, monitor off, still not gone home. Out of breath. Then one evening by the window, looking across at the other buildings — every one of them has a window brighter than the rest. Best in this room, ordinary in the next. The finish line he'd chased for two years kept moving with every room. He stopped racing. The next morning he still opened his laptop and did his work.

![Moment 3](/characters/16-09-2026-van-phong-3.png)

A meeting. The boss praises the new guy. He used to go home after that and lie there staring at the ceiling, streetlight cutting across it, unable to sleep. Now he hears it and is glad for him — not pretending. The kid being good doesn't make him worse. He claps, goes back to his desk, picks up what he left half done. Might even slide a coffee over.

> The middle isn't where the losers stand.
> It's where nearly everyone does.

He still tries every day. He just stopped punishing himself for not being first. And you — when was the last time you lost sleep over praise that went to someone else?
