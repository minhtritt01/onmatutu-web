Back in January he sat down for maybe five minutes and wrote out the things he wanted to do this year. Then he pinned it to the wall and felt lighter, as if writing it down were enough to make it happen. It's August now. The paper is still there, the bottom edge curling up.

![Moment 1](/characters/01-08-2026-chung-1.png)

That night a friend posted something — everything on the list, done. Nice photo, few words, a green check underneath. He read it twice, then thought about his own lines still hanging on the wall. No envy, really. Just a second of feeling slow. Except nobody posts the part that isn't finished. What the feed shows him is a filtered scoreboard, and what he's holding up against it is all seven of his actual months, including the ones with nothing to report. A comparison like that was never going to come out even.

![Moment 2](/characters/01-08-2026-chung-2.png)

The next morning he stood at the fridge and looked at the note longer than usual. The ink had faded, the edges gone yellow. And it landed: the person who wrote those lines was him in January — someone who didn't know yet how these seven months would go, didn't know about the weeks where showing up for work was the whole budget, didn't know about the mornings that started already tired. That person spent five minutes on it. He's spent seven months living it. So he took the note down. Didn't tear it up — just folded it and put it in a drawer.

![Moment 3](/characters/01-08-2026-chung-3.png)

Then one afternoon over coffee a friend asked the ordinary question: so what have you done this year? He went quiet. Honestly, nothing to report. No award, no certificate, no trip. But sitting with it a moment longer, he remembered there was one thing he'd done for all seven months without missing a day — he went to work. That one isn't on anybody's list, nobody writes it down, nobody claps, so nobody counts it as having done something.

> Nothing worth showing off, honestly.
> But I made it through seven months.

Five months left in the year. He hasn't promised himself anything new yet. If someone asked you that question right now, which days would you count?
