Late at night, a small rented room, nothing lit but the screen throwing light back up at his
face. He didn't mean to pick up the phone. It was just in his hand. The first thing on the
feed is an old friend, standing in front of a new house.

![Moment 1](/characters/14-08-2026-chung-1.png)

Twenty-five, an ordinary salary, two years to put aside one small amount. He knows exactly
what that number is — it's in the notebook on his desk, added up month by month in his own
handwriting. But nothing scrolling past him has a number attached. Only pictures. He keeps
going for a while, then stops: the one who posted every month hasn't posted anything in weeks.

![Moment 2](/characters/14-08-2026-chung-2.png)

Last month, at a sidewalk coffee place, that same friend sat across from him and talked about
what he'd made. The new bike was parked right outside, under a streetlight that had just come
on. He listened, and drank until the ice was down to one piece. Walking past this month, the
bike is still there — with a handwritten sheet of paper taped to the windshield, one strip of
tape at each corner.

![Moment 3](/characters/14-08-2026-chung-3.png)

Tonight he opens his banking app. The number is short enough that he doesn't need to read it
carefully. Then a message comes in. The person who was ahead of him is asking to borrow, and
he's the one who has it on hand. He sends it, doesn't write anything else, doesn't tell
anyone. The notebook is still open on the desk — twenty-four columns, all about the same
height, not one of them short.

> Being slower than someone else isn't losing.
> Everyone's clock runs at its own speed.

The little plant in the clay pot on the windowsill grows month by month too, slowly enough
that nobody has ever photographed it. Whose clock have you been measuring yourself against?
