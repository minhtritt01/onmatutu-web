At dinner last night her mother reached over and put food in her bowl again. Same as every night: the good pieces end up on her side, and when she finally looks across the table her mother's bowl has nothing in it but greens and a little broth. There are only two of them now. There is food left over. The bowl looks the same anyway.

![Moment 1](/characters/08-09-2026-chung-4.png)

One evening she walked into the kitchen earlier than usual and saw a small plate set off to one side — the good part, already picked out, before anyone had sat down. So the giving had never happened at the table at all. It was finished back in the kitchen, every day, somewhere she never looked. That night she reached across and put something in her mother's bowl, for the first time.

![Moment 2](/characters/08-09-2026-chung-5.png)

She has come home late for two years now. Ten at night, the food still covered, the rice still warm, and she kept thinking she was just lucky. Then one night she walked into the kitchen and the stove was still warm, with a few drops of water not yet dried at the bottom of the pot — the mark of a third reheating. Nobody ever mentions that part. You reheat it, you clean up, you go to bed.

![Moment 3](/characters/08-09-2026-chung-6.png)

There is a version of this at work too. Every lunch there is one extra box on the table, and on the days she forgets hers, that box is hers. She assumed her deskmate ordered it for her — until she took a day off and heard it was still sitting there. It had never had anyone's name on it. It was for whoever forgot.

> I thought I was getting the good part.
> Turns out I was the one being chosen.

Nobody in these three stories did anything remarkable. A plate set aside, a pot reheated, one extra lunch — repeated so steadily that the person receiving it mistakes it for the natural order of things. At your table tonight, is someone quietly doing one of these?
