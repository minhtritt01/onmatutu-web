Night, a dark rented room, only the phone screen lit. Rent is due and pay hasn't landed, so he
types the same message asking for a loan over and over, swapping a word here and there so it
sounds less embarrassing, then sends it to nine people. Then he flips the phone face down —
staring at it won't make anyone answer faster.

![Moment 1](/characters/21-09-2026-chung-1.png)

Morning: eight chats, eight "seen", nothing underneath. All family — older siblings, aunts,
uncles, the people who've known him since he was small. The ninth message went to a high-school
friend he hadn't seen in five years, sent just to round out the list. He's the only one who wrote
back: "take this for now." Family calculates. A friend just hands it over. He takes the money and
asks the friend out for coffee — the first time in five years.

![Moment 2](/characters/21-09-2026-chung-2.png)

At work, the friend at the next desk turns and asks if he's okay. He says he's fine — he's seven
million short this month and his own family hasn't helped, so who else would he tell. At noon the
phone buzzes: the friend has transferred exactly seven million. The exact figure from the phone
call in the corridor that morning. He said "fine" so he wouldn't owe anyone; the friend lent it
so he wouldn't have to hide. That afternoon he pulls his chair over and tells the whole thing.

![Moment 3](/characters/21-09-2026-chung-3.png)

Out of work for a month and short on cash, he texts his older brother. The reply is "let me
think about it" — and everyone knows what that means. On Sunday at the pitch, a friend he only
ever sees at football pushes three million through the fence without a question. Just kicking a
ball around together, and that was enough for him to care. The next week he shows up early.

> Eight relatives read it and went quiet.
> Five years apart, a friend still remembered.

Who is your ninth person — the one you didn't dare count on when you typed the message, and the
only one who answered?
