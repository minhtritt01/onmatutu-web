A customer snaps at him through the headset and he still says yes, of course. A colleague says something sharp and he still smiles. A friend asks a favour at nine at night and he still nods. All day long, nobody sees him lose his patience once.

Then the front door opens.

![Moment 1](/characters/26-08-2026-chung-1.png)

The work bag lands on a chair, the lanyard slides to the floor and stays there. From the kitchen his mother asks the most ordinary question in the world: have you eaten yet. The answer comes out before he can think about it — clipped, closed, the kind meant to end a conversation. She says a small *ừ* and goes back to the dishes without asking again. It's only later, in the kitchen, that he sees the tray under the mesh cover, still warm. She'd reheated it hours ago. The question was never a question.

![Moment 2](/characters/26-08-2026-chung-2.png)

Ten at night, bedroom door shut. Out there he agrees to anything anyone asks; in here his father asks one thing and he says nothing. His father knocks once, then stops. He lies in the dark listening to the footsteps go quiet and feels like he's won something that isn't worth winning. In the morning there's a glass of warm water on a small saucer, set down right at the threshold. His father wasn't waiting for the door to open. He just came back, while nobody was watching.

![Moment 3](/characters/26-08-2026-chung-3.png)

The next night he starts typing an apology to his mother. He gets to the third draft and his thumb stops over send, deletes, starts again. While he's still stuck there, the screen lights up first — she's texted to say she's making soup tomorrow. Nothing about yesterday. She'd forgiven him long before he found the words. And being forgiven that fast is exactly the thing he's learned to spend.

> The patience I save for strangers —
> she deserves the same. Just the same.

Home is the rare place where you get to be at your worst and still not be left. That's luck, and it's also the easiest kind of luck to spend without noticing. Try one small thing this week: give your parents exactly as much patience as you already hand to strangers. Not more. When was the last time you held your tongue for someone you'll never see again?
