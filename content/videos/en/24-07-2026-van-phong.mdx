The meeting ends. She sits still, saying nothing. Her boss doesn't scold her — just asks, gently, "What do you think went wrong?" Somehow that question lands heavier than being yelled at ever would.

![Moment 1](/characters/24-07-2026-van-phong-1.png)

Back at her desk, she tries to remember exactly where it went wrong. No one's asking her to have the answer right now — she just can't sit still without asking herself.

![Moment 2](/characters/24-07-2026-van-phong-2.png)

Days later, she sits replaying her calls with customers, thinking she should have said things differently. Everyone in this line of work hits a rough patch — not just her. Knowing that doesn't stop her from blaming herself anyway.

![Moment 3](/characters/24-07-2026-van-phong-3.png)

One night, with no one asking her to, she opens her laptop and starts rewriting her plan from scratch. Not for anyone to review. She just wants to prove something to herself first.

> Not good enough yet isn't failure.
> It's just where she starts again.

It's not that she wasn't trying. This time, she's choosing to start over for herself first, without waiting for anyone to assign it. Have you ever sat down like that — not because anyone made you, but because you wanted to try again on your own terms?
