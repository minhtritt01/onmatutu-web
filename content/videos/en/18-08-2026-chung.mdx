Three weeks since he last called home. Not out of anger, and not because he was that busy either. Just that every night the phone stayed face down on the table one more time, and he promised himself he'd call tomorrow.

![Moment 1](/characters/18-08-2026-chung-1.png)

His mother texted every week. Short things — have you eaten, is it cold up there. Some nights he opened his contacts, tapped two digits, then stopped and let the screen go dark. It took him a while to understand something: she was hesitating too. She texted instead of calling because she was afraid he'd be in a meeting, on a deadline, worn out. The two people who loved each other most in that family, sitting at opposite ends of the city, each waiting for the other to dial first.

![Moment 2](/characters/18-08-2026-chung-2.png)

With the person he loved, the silence took a different shape. Three years together, and every argument ended the same way — each of them retreating to a separate room. In the morning both got up early, made coffee, talked normally as if nothing had happened. He used to think that was maturity, not holding grudges. Until the day he counted back and realised: three years, and not once had they talked anything all the way through. What he'd taken for peace was really a pile nobody had cleared.

![Moment 3](/characters/18-08-2026-chung-3.png)

This last one is the hardest to see, because it looks nothing like neglect. Flowers on Women's Day, every year. Never a birthday missed. Anniversaries at somewhere nice. Living under one roof, seeing each other daily — and still feeling far away. Dinner together every night with two phones propped up like little walls. What had gone missing wasn't the effort. The big occasions had quietly taken the place of reaching for someone's hand for no reason at all.

> Flowers bloom once a year.
> Holding a hand is every day.

Tonight he dialled home, put the phone down afterwards, and sat a little closer. Nothing got resolved — it just started again. And you: the last time you did one small thing for someone you love, with no occasion attached, when was it?
