Quarterly review. The lights dim, the projector lights up a name. She sits in the back row
and claps with everyone else. Steady hands, ordinary face. The thing on the screen is what
she spent months building — but the name read out belongs to the team lead, the one who
signed off on it.

She doesn't need a round of applause. Honestly, she just needs one person to know it was
hers. That's all.

![Moment 1](/characters/15-09-2026-van-phong-1.png)

Walking out of the hall, she notices something stranger than being left off the slide: she
never wrote it down anywhere either. No email, no note, not a single line. A few months from
now, other people forgetting is one thing — but she'll forget too. When she did it, what it
changed, the actual numbers. Waiting for someone to know, when she never let it exist
anywhere for anyone to find.

![Moment 2](/characters/15-09-2026-van-phong-2.png)

Back at her desk, she opens a new file. Names it *Things I've Done*. Writes it all in —
what, when, what came of it, the exact figures. Not to file a complaint. Not to show off.
It's just that last time she sat in a salary conversation and they asked what she'd
accomplished, she couldn't get it out. Not because there was nothing to say — because there
was nowhere to pull it from.

![Moment 3](/characters/15-09-2026-van-phong-3.png)

End of the month, she opens the file and adds one more line. A thank-you can go to the wrong
person. The work itself can't. She keeps the numbers, and lets the numbers do the talking
when it counts.

> Nobody said your name — that doesn't make you bad at this.
> Keep the numbers. Let the numbers speak for you.

Do you have a file called *Things I've Done*? Or are you still waiting for someone else to
remember for you?
