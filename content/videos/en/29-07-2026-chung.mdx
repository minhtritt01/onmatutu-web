11pm, the phone screen still glowing. The message to a partner runs a whole page, typed
without a second thought. Scroll up, and the thread with mom is just a few short lines,
the last one sent three days ago.

![Moment 1](/characters/29-07-2026-chung-1.png)

It's not about being busy. It's habit — the quiet certainty that she'll always be there,
always waiting, always forgiving the silence. So the words of love just sit inside,
saved for some "later" that never actually arrives.

![Moment 2](/characters/29-07-2026-chung-2.png)

She calls in the middle of a busy afternoon, just to ask if he's eaten. The reply is
three words: "yeah," "I know," "busy." She doesn't get angry. She just waits quietly,
like always — and somehow that quiet weighs more than any complaint could.

![Moment 3](/characters/29-07-2026-chung-3.png)

Tonight, he tries something small: opens the chat with her, types one line — nothing
fancy, just true. His hand shakes a little. He hits send anyway, and something in his
chest loosens — not because the words were eloquent, but because they were real.

> Parents don't need pretty words.
> One honest line is warm enough.

Maybe she never needed a long, perfectly worded message. Just one line, at the right
moment, is enough to warm a whole year. Have you texted her tonight?
