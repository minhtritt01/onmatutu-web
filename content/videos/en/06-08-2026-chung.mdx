Late night, and the only light in the rented room is the screen. She opens the old chat
with her best friend from high school — the one where they used to talk until two in the
morning, about everything, scrolling forever without reaching the start. Then she scrolls
to the bottom. Today is one line.

![Moment 1](/characters/06-08-2026-chung-1.png)

They once promised they'd stay close forever. Now one of them is in Hà Nội and the other
in Sài Gòn, and messages that used to fill a page have shrunk to "Yeah." She almost blames
her friend, then looks again: that last clipped word was hers. Nobody cooled off first —
they were both waiting for the other one to say something.

![Moment 2](/characters/06-08-2026-chung-2.png)

Another evening, she scrolls past a photo of her friend out with a new group. She's glad,
honestly — and still a little hollow, because she doesn't recognise anyone in it. Then she
opens her own album, and her friend wouldn't recognise anyone either. They've both built a
life the other one isn't in. Nobody did anything wrong.

![Moment 3](/characters/06-08-2026-chung-3.png)

Tonight she types a message and deletes it. Types it again, deletes it again — afraid of
getting a polite one-word reply. Right then, "typing…" appears on the other side, and then
it stops. They were both nervous, so they were both quiet. In the end she hits send without
rereading it, and puts the phone down.

> A shorter message isn't a colder one.
> Still wanting to text is still being close.

Some friendships don't break loudly. They just quietly stretch in two directions.
Your friend from back then — when was the last time you sent them anything?
