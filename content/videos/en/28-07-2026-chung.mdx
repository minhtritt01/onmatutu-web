7am, the yard still damp with dew. She wheels an old bicycle out to the gate, her
7-year-old grandson already seated behind her, arms wrapped tight around her back. His
parents work in a factory town far away, and come home only once a year.

![Moment 1](/characters/28-07-2026-chung-1.png)

She never finished more than the fourth grade, but every night she sits beside him,
sounding out each word slowly. No one asked her to. She simply decided this was hers to
carry, even though a pen sits stranger in her hand than a hoe ever did.

![Moment 2](/characters/28-07-2026-chung-2.png)

One evening he asked why his parents weren't home. She just smiled, ran a hand over his
hair, and said they were working to pay for his school. That night, alone on the porch,
she called her son — just to tell him the teacher had praised the boy that day. Her voice
brightened, as if she were the one being praised.

![Moment 3](/characters/28-07-2026-chung-3.png)

She hung up and went back inside to clean, laying out breakfast for the morning. No one
asked if she was tired. But watching him sleep soundly beside her, she thought: as long
as he's alright, being tired doesn't matter.

> No one has asked if she's tired all day.
> His peace is enough to make her well.

Somewhere, a grandmother or grandfather is quietly carrying a piece of someone else's
life, asking for nothing in return. Next time you call home, will you ask them how they're
really doing?
