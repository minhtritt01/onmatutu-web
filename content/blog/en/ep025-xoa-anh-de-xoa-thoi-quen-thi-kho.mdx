Ngân, 23, broke up two months ago. She deleted the shared photos, unfollowed him, cleared out everything he'd left in her room — all in the first week. She thought that was that.

---

But every night at exactly 10:30pm — the time they used to video call, every single day for a year and a half — her hand still reaches for her phone on its own. Not because she misses him. Her hand just hasn't caught up yet.

That night at 10:32, halfway through a show, Ngân caught herself picking up her phone to check the lock screen. No call, obviously. She put it down. At 10:35, she picked it up again. She laughed at herself: *"Come on, girl."*

---

Deleting a photo takes a second. Deleting a phone number takes one tap. But a habit repeated across 548 nights doesn't disappear just because you decided it should. Ngân knows that now — and instead of scolding herself for not being "over it" yet, she just noted it: night sixty-one, and her hand still remembers. That's fine.

---

A year and a half of habit doesn't fade as fast as a photo disappears. But it does fade. Not today — but it fades.

***"Healing doesn't have a deadline. Slow down — the habit will fade."***

**Are you rushing yourself to forget faster than you actually can?**
