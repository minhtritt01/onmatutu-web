Almost 11pm on a Monday, Khôi, 21, is about to turn off the light and sleep in his rented room when he stops — counting on his fingers, it's been 9 days since he called home.

---

This week has been packed: a project deadline, three midterms back to back, plus two extra evening shifts. His mother hadn't called either — she knew he was busy and didn't want to bother him. Three years living apart, and they'd both gotten used to giving each other space.

---

Khôi dials. Barely one ring, and his mother picks up, her voice alert despite the late hour. *"Yeah, it's you. Why so late?"* They talk for exactly five minutes — she asks if he's eating well, he asks how his father's doing. Nothing big, just small things.

---

She says, *"Alright, go to sleep, it's late,"* then hangs up. Khôi lies back down, feeling lighter — not because he said anything important, just hearing her voice was enough. Nothing more needed.

---

***"Call home. I don't need you to tell me much. Just knowing you're okay is enough."***
